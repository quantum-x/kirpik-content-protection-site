An apparel store can reshoot its photography in an afternoon and replace a supplier in a season. The text on its product pages is harder to replace, because the best of it is not really writing. It is fit knowledge, collected from fittings, returns and customer emails, then set down so the next shopper orders the right size first time. That is the text clone stores take, and they take it because they cannot produce it themselves.

## What Fit Notes and Size Guidance carry

Read a strong apparel page and count the decisions in it. The fit note says whether the cut runs large and which way to go between sizes. The model line gives a height and the size worn, so the photographs can be judged against a real body. The size chart separates garment measurements from body measurements, which is the difference between a jacket that fits and a jacket that goes back in the post. The fabric line states weight and composition, 320 gsm brushed loopback cotton rather than "premium fabric", because weight tells a shopper what the fabric is like. The care note admits that the linen creases, and says so before the first complaint says it less kindly.

Every one of those lines earns its keep twice. It converts the shopper who was unsure, and it heads off the return from the shopper who would otherwise have guessed. For an apparel business, where returns decide margins, honest sizing text is operational equipment as much as it is marketing.

None of it can be written from a supplier catalogue or a sample photograph. A seller who has never held the garment cannot tell anyone how it drapes, whether the rise sits high or the sleeve runs long, or what happens to the black after twenty washes. That seller is precisely the one who needs your description.

## How apparel text reaches a clone store

The routes are few and well worn. A lookalike garment comes off a production line close enough to the original to pass in photographs, and its seller lifts the original description whole, because honest fit text cannot be written without the garment. A dropshipper importing the same factory item takes the description of whichever store ranks best for it. A marketplace seller pastes it into a listing form because the form demands text and yours converts.

Almost none of this is done by hand. Shopify publishes every store's product text in machine-readable form that scraping tools read directly, without ever opening the storefront; [every store has a public product feed and it cannot be turned off](/learn/shopify-public-product-feed/). Pulling a catalogue of titles, descriptions and variants takes minutes, which is why stolen apparel text so often keeps the original line breaks, the model's height and, in careless cases, the original brand name mid-sentence.

The fashion calendar sharpens the damage. A drop earns most of its revenue while the trend is live, so a duplicate listing that appears two weeks into that window competes for the searches you created at the exact moment they are worth the most. A takedown that lands after the season ends protects nothing. Whatever protection an apparel store relies on has to produce usable evidence in days, not months.

## Make every drop provable

Text on a public page can always be copied, and no app changes that. What a store does control is whether the duplicate can be traced. An invisible text watermark places the store's identity inside the text itself: an algorithmic invisible watermark, derived from your original text's signature, that changes nothing a shopper or a search engine sees. Whoever takes the description takes the watermark with it, and a version that has been trimmed, restyled or lightly reworded still identifies its owner.

The routine fits the way apparel stores already work. Protect a drop's products when you publish them, in the same pass as the final check on the size chart. Finding a clone remains a human job, and the reliable method is unglamorous: search for one distinctive sizing line from your description in quotation marks. Thieves edit headlines; they rarely edit a fit note, because they cannot tell whether the edit would make it wrong.
