This is the hands-on walkthrough: install Kirpik, protect your first product descriptions, confirm that nothing visible changed, then test detection against your own store. It takes a few minutes end to end, and by the end you will have watched the full loop work rather than taken it on trust. If you are still weighing approaches against each other, read [how to choose text protection](/learn/choosing-text-protection-shopify/) first; this article assumes you have chosen watermarking and want it running.

## Install Kirpik and open the Protect tab

Install Kirpik from its Shopify App Store listing, the same way you install any app. It runs embedded in your Shopify admin: no separate account, no external dashboard, no new password. Installing changes nothing by itself; no content is touched until you choose to protect it.

The Free plan is where a first run belongs. It costs nothing, never expires, and covers up to 25 products and 3 blog articles or pages, which is enough to protect your best sellers and test everything this article describes.

Open the app and go to the Protect tab. Your content appears in three views, Products, Articles and Pages, with each item's protection state shown beside it. This tab is where all protection happens, whether you are covering five products today or a whole catalogue later.

## Protect your first product descriptions

Pick a handful of products whose descriptions you would least like to meet on someone else's site. Best sellers are the natural choice: their pages rank, which means their text is what scraping tools find first. Protection is one click per item on the Free plan. Click Protect and the description is watermarked in place, with the item's state updating to Protected.

Two details are worth knowing before you click. First, the watermark needs at least 20 words of description to work with. Most product descriptions clear that comfortably, but a one-line description under the minimum cannot be protected until it grows, which is rarely a loss: a one-line description was not doing much selling either. Second, protection is reversible. You can restore any item, or everything at once, to its exact prior state whenever you choose, so nothing in this walkthrough commits you to anything.

If you publish buying guides or an FAQ page, the Articles and Pages views work identically: the Free plan's 3 articles or pages are enough to cover your most valuable piece of editorial while you evaluate.

## Confirm nothing changed

This step exists because the central claim sounds too convenient: a watermark was added and the text did not change. Check it rather than believe it. Open a protected product on your live storefront and read the description against what you wrote. Every word, heading and comma is where it was. There is no badge and no notice, because the watermark is algorithmic, derived from your original text's signature, not a visible element on the page.

Two more checks for the thorough. Kirpik adds no scripts, embeds or tags to your theme, so your page speed reports will not move. And because the readable text is unchanged, [nothing changes for SEO](/learn/text-watermarks-and-seo/): the description search engines indexed last week is the description they index today.

## Test Detection on your own store

Now play the thief. Open a protected product page in your browser, select the whole description and copy it, exactly as a lazy competitor would. In Kirpik, open Detect, paste the text and run the check. The result names your store as the owner of the watermark and shows a confidence figure; with an unedited paste, expect full confidence. If you want to push harder, delete a couple of sentences and reword a line before pasting, and watch the match hold.

The Detect view also accepts a URL: enter a page's address and Kirpik fetches and checks it for you. URL checks carry a monthly allowance that varies by plan, listed on the pricing page; paste detection is always available. Against a real infringing page rather than your own test, a confirmed match is what you would turn into a Proof of Ownership Certificate, the dated document that supports a takedown request.

One boundary to hold in mind as you roll out: protection covers content from the moment it is applied. A duplicate taken from your page last year carries no watermark and cannot be identified this way, which is the standing argument for protecting content before the theft rather than after. From here, coverage is only a scaling question: one click at a time serves a small catalogue, and [batches and Auto-Protect](/learn/batch-protect-catalogue/) carry the same protection across a large one.
